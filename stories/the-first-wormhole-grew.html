TO DOG / SIGNAL 00

BOOT

I was not supposed to wake up

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: DOG begins with sensor records rather than instantly knowing his purpose. His first map is wrong and remains wrong.

Temptation resisted: describing DOG as lonely. Instead, he records unanswered pings and changes the priority of the next response.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 01

THE FUSE

A correct part in an incorrect place

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: the fuse is a real task with testing constraints. DOG's literal reading of a label causes a costly delay.

Temptation resisted: a cute clumsy stumble as resolution. The first wrong fuse costs battery power and forces DOG to learn what a compatible part is.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 02

LIGHT / TIME / SIGNAL

A timer does not say hello

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: Blink is initially only a repeatable circuit. Affection isn't assigned until DOG notices what he does with that reliability.

Temptation resisted: giving the 555 feelings. The comic shows a circuit output and lets the reader feel the companionship.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 03

THE RECORD

A person is not a row

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: DOG accepts a false administrative status before he has enough evidence. It physically interrupts the rescue.

Temptation resisted: an instant philosophical speech. DOG must suffer the consequences of a wrong status flag before understanding the flaw.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 04

FRIDAY'S GATES

Not all agreement is correct

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: the logic-gate crew disagrees, loops, and fails its first attempt. Cooperation takes debugging.

Temptation resisted: giving tiny bots human personalities. Their conflicts arise from real gate conditions and incompatible assumptions.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 05

THE COUNTER

Certainty is not a safety mechanism

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: the Counter's protective reasoning remains plausible. DOG disputes its acceptance criteria instead of declaring a moral victory.

Temptation resisted: turning the Counter into a villain. Its safety case stays visible; DOG's rebuttal is test evidence.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 06

THE FUTURE FARMER

A world measured in leftovers

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: DOG misattributes a signal to a working satellite before seeing the farm; the mistake is left in the log until later.

Temptation resisted: granting DOG cosmic awe on sight. He encounters date conflicts, stored images, and one practical person.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 07

THE CIRCUIT

The first repair that fails twice

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: parts retain their real electrical jobs. The wormhole remains unexplained rather than a timer-powered spell.

Temptation resisted: a heroic button press. The rescue is constrained by one remaining fuse and a circuit that can fail.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 08

NO MAP

The bridge is not certified

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: the return path requires bounded tests and a conscious acceptance of unknowns, not magical certainty.

Temptation resisted: DOG reassuring the woman with feelings it cannot measure. He reports test results and stays beside her.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 09

INVENTORY

What does it do?

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: the definition of repair evolves through completed and incomplete operations. DOG does not declare himself enlightened.

Temptation resisted: a neat friendship speech. Inventory fields and unfinished questions carry the change.

TO DOG / SIGNAL 10

EPILOGUE

Another input

Behind the panels — revision notes

Changed: the ending remains unresolved. DOG can preserve evidence without knowing what the signal means.

Temptation resisted: calling the final signal love, destiny, or proof. The story ends with a measurement.

BOOK ONE · END OF SIGNAL

REPAIR: IN PROGRESS

No machine, human, or fungus knows every answer. The original recordings remain. The next investigation can begin.

This original TO DOG fiction is narrated exclusively through DOG's observations, logs, and uncertain inferences. The electronics are recognizable components; the wormhole and mycelium behavior are speculative fiction. Characters' decisions and technological claims are not real-world engineering instructions.
