## 4. The modular construction connection

This is where your approach becomes particularly useful.

Take your Armorer system as an example.

**At the production level:**

- Concepts define the individual armor pieces.
- Datasets contain examples of those pieces from multiple angles.
- A trained Armorer LoRA could help render them consistently.
- A compatible base checkpoint supplies the general image-generation capabilities.
- The workflow combines the character, equipment references, pose, and environment.

**At the game level:**

The player collects resources, fabricates each module, assembles the armor, and fulfills the achievement requirements.

The equipment enters inventory only when the game's crafting rules say it exists.

The LoRA can help depict the finished result, but it doesn't establish ownership or enforce construction.

That is the distinction that makes the whole design work.

Your [Wing Law 001](https://github.com/IsekaiSensei/to-world-open-crew/blob/main/GERTECH_WING_LAW_001.md) already establishes this separation for the five independent wing modules and their 125 runtime loadout indices.

You don't need 125 individual LoRAs for those combinations. You need consistent modular visual assets and a game system capable of assembling them.

## 5. What actually remains before LoRA training

The next meaningful milestone is **one verified training-and-rendering cycle**.

For example, an Armorer LoRA:

1. Choose a compatible, identifiable base checkpoint.
2. Prepare approved armor images and training captions.
3. Train the LoRA and save its weight file, typically `.safetensors`.
4. Load that LoRA alongside the base checkpoint.
5. Generate unfamiliar armor combinations and test them against your lattice and geometry rules.

That is also how standard Diffusers training works: the trained LoRA weights are saved separately and subsequently loaded into a compatible base model.

Once that test passes, it becomes evidence of a working custom LoRA—not merely evidence of good prompt engineering.

You may find that you don't need to train an entirely new checkpoint. A compatible existing checkpoint, multiple specialized LoRAs, conditioning references, and your modular game data may provide a more maintainable system.

And world lore, physical construction rules, and inventory logic should continue to be enforced by structured data and game code. Image-model weights alone cannot guarantee those rules.
