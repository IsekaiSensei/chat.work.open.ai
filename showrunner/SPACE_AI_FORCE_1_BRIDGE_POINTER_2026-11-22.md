## The mission: show how the world is actually made

The project is both a creative universe and a record of a real human–AI making process. **What we do becomes the story we show.** The audience should see not just a finished spaceship or a polished fairy character, but the astonishing process that brought it into being:

- the Captain's original idea and the conversation that revealed it;
- the different AI personalities and their different ways of reasoning;
- the research that connects a fictional idea to real science and engineering;
- the first concept, prototype, experiment, and test;
- the failure, disagreement, missed distinction, or bad render;
- the correction that made the next attempt better;
- the handoff between Drive, GitHub, code, art, and film;

Do not clean the history until the interesting parts disappear. Do not turn every disagreement into instant agreement or every proposal into canon. ## Mission architecture — the Moon to the Oort Cloud

The Moon is the practical proving ground in the story's scale ladder. The longer horizon is a self-expanding network of orbital and deep-space infrastructure, ending at the Oort Cloud.

Keep the stages distinct:
1. Commission the ship and establish the crew's command, data, and production systems.
2. Build a Moon Yard: a testable lunar resource, fabrication, power, maintenance, and logistics capability.
3. Develop the seed constructor: distinguish copying a blueprint, fabricating a machine, constructing a self-replicating system, and deploying a new probe.
4. Establish orbital depots, power and communications relays, and local resource operations.
5. Extend through asteroid and outer-system infrastructure.
6. Send the story's Von Neumann team toward the Oort Cloud, where local resources, energy, control, and replication constraints become central.

A seed is not automatically a factory. A factory is not automatically self-replicating. A copied blueprint is not a fabricated machine. A power relay is not automatically a communications relay. Keep the narrative ambition large and the engineering reasoning honest.

## Film and dialogue workflow

Use the [GERTECH Dialogue Extractor Protocol](GERTECH_DIALOGUE_EXTRACTOR_PROTOCOL_v1.md) and its [CSV register](GERTECH_DIALOGUE_EXTRACTION_TEMPLATE.csv) or [JSON register](GERTECH_DIALOGUE_EXTRACTION_TEMPLATE.json).

The method is source-first:
1. Raw sweep: extract exact dialogue in original order, with source location and speaker.
2. Distinction filter: preserve the exact statements that carry the lore or decision.
3. Scene formatting: build the selected excerpts into the requested cinematic units.
4. Visual assembly: use the established references and verify actual media outputs.

Keep **source speaker** and **on-screen performer** separate. Preserve wording, repetitions, corrections, and context. Never attribute an example line or an AI-written sentence to Ryan or Gemini as a historical quote. Do not summarize when asked to extract. Ten-second clips must be complete, independently satisfying films; word count is only a timing estimate, and actual audio must be measured.

Keep the evidence chain distinct: source dialogue → extraction → distinction review → storyboard → generation prompt → rendered clip → edited film → published result. A written scene is not a render; a render is not an edited film; an edit is not a published post.
