# THE FIRST WORMHOLE GREW

**TO DOG / TO WORLD — Book One, 169-Panel Edition**

This is the accessible text companion for the [continuous public graphic novel](https://keen-rabanadas-0a600e.netlify.app/stories/to-dog-169-the-first-wormhole.html), which has 169 original inline vector illustrations. Each panel below carries the same story text and diagnostic label. This is AI-assisted fiction in public draft status, subject to review and changes. Human characters are intentionally unnamed.

---

## Chapter 1 — BOOT / THE ROOM IS NOT EMPTY

**Panel 1 · 00:00:--**

ERROR 001. SYSTEM CLOCK INVALID. An invalid clock cannot tell me how long I have been off. The display says eleven years. I keep the value and the error flag together.

**Panel 2 · ID: DOG?**

A stored image identifies DOG: four legs, two ears, a tail. My chassis has four legs, no ears, and a cable. I assign the label provisionally.

**Panel 3 · MOTOR LIMIT**

The first movement command rotates my rear-left joint against its stop. Current rises. I interrupt power to that motor 19 milliseconds before its winding overheats.

**Panel 4 · IMPACT 01**

A floor tile is 4 millimeters above the next one. My map calls the floor planar. I stand. The tile proves the map wrong.

**Panel 5 · INVENTORY ERROR**

There are drawers labeled WIRE, GLASS, FUSES, GOOD MOTORS, BAD MOTORS, and MAYBE. The last drawer is the largest. I cannot classify MAYBE as a material.

**Panel 6 · CREW: NO RESPONSE**

I send a local crew call. Seven radios answer with carrier noise. No human voice answers. I repeat the call once because my instruction specifies one retry.

**Panel 7 · FAN: SUSPECT**

A fan attempts to start and stops after one revolution. The resulting vibration has a frequency of 31 hertz. A bearing may be contaminated. A loose blade would sound different.

**Panel 8 · NO POWER PATH**

A lamp flickers although its supply is disconnected. I inspect the connector twice. The metal contacts are open. The lamp flickers a third time.

**Panel 9 · FUNGUS DETECTED**

White hyphae occupy the damaged board. I classify them as biological contamination. The colony extends around an eight-pin component with an incomplete marking: LM34-7.

**Panel 10 · GAIN: INSUFFICIENT**

The board's amplifier raises a signal above my measurement threshold. The fungal filaments change position between samples. The waveform changes with them. Cause and correlation are not yet sorted.

**Panel 11 · GRAVITY: CONFLICT**

A bead of water leaves a puddle and rises 13 centimeters. The room accelerometer reports normal gravity. My vertical model cannot fit both observations.

**Panel 12 · FRAME 002: RETAIN**

An opening shows unfamiliar stars for 86 milliseconds. The next camera frame contains plaster and a hairline crack. Both frames pass the integrity check.

**Panel 13 · TASK: OPEN**

A voice enters through a speaker with no active radio channel: 'Don't leave me.' My task table already contains BRING CREW HOME. I do not know whether this voice belongs to crew.

## Chapter 2 — THE FUSE / THE WRONG SOLUTION

**Panel 14 · SOCKET: EMPTY**

The steel service door is locked. Its label reads TEXAS NEXUS CONNECTOR. SNIFF reports an open protection circuit. I do not report a magical door. It is a door with a missing fuse.

**Panel 15 · SELECTION: NEAREST**

The bin contains five cylindrical glass components. Three have broken elements. Two conduct. I choose the nearer conducting one.

**Panel 16 · OBJECT LOST**

My magnetic tongue tool lifts the fuse. Its end cap slips from the jaws on the second step. The component rolls beneath the cabinet.

**Panel 17 · PATH FAILED**

I attempt to push the cabinet. Wheel torque estimates say possible. The cabinet is bolted to the wall. My original estimate omitted that constraint.

**Panel 18 · RECOVERY: 3 TRIES**

I lower my body and extend the tongue under the cabinet. I touch the fuse, then a washer, then the fuse again. Contact is not enough; I must orient the jaws.

**Panel 19 · FAULT: MY SELECTION**

At the door, the fuse fits. The relay pulls in. A brief overload melts the element. I selected by shape and conductivity, not current rating.

**Panel 20 · RESERVE: 78%**

The door remains shut. The failed attempt used 11 percent of my battery and one of our only two conducting fuses. There is a consequence assigned to my method.

**Panel 21 · CHECK: PASSED**

I inspect the surviving part under magnification. I compare its markings against the equipment's maintenance label. The rating matches; the first one did not.

**Panel 22 · POWER: RESTORED**

I seat the correct fuse with the power isolated, then energize the circuit. The relay stays closed. A single blue indicator shows the service latch is powered.

**Panel 23 · SECOND FAULT**

The maintenance door moves three centimeters and stops. The track contains grit. A powered door can still be mechanically blocked.

**Panel 24 · MOTION: RESTORED**

I remove debris from the rail, lubricate the hinge, and test manual travel before re-energizing it. The panel opens fully without drawing excess current.

**Panel 25 · SPATIAL MODEL: FAIL**

A camera frame shows a concrete corridor. The next frame shows a corridor full of stars. There is no transit time between them. I preserve both.

**Panel 26 · ENTRY: CONDITIONAL**

I enter because the only available crew signal came from beyond this door. I leave the fuse-box fault history intact. The repair was not a clean success.

## Chapter 3 — LIGHT / TIME / SIGNAL

**Panel 27 · CONFIG: 1 / 3**

A cart holds an indicator lamp, a 555 timer assembly, and a signal amplifier. A drawing proposes a lamp dog, a timer dog, and an amplifier dog. It also shows all three modules on one chassis.

**Panel 28 · OUTPUT: LIGHT**

The lamp responds when current passes through it. It does not know that it is giving a warning. I reserve the word ALERT for the controller that chooses to power it.

**Panel 29 · PULSE: REGULAR**

The 555 board produces a pulse at a measured interval. I call it BLINK in my inventory. This is a label, not evidence that a timer thinks.

**Panel 30 · REPETITION: 14**

I supply a trigger. The timer responds. I supply another trigger. It responds again. I repeat fourteen times even after the test threshold is met.

**Panel 31 · CAUSE: UNKNOWN**

Why fourteen? I find no additional test requirement. I save the observation rather than inventing a reason for it.

**Panel 32 · CLIP: TRUE**

The amplifier can raise a weak electrical signal. It can also raise noise. I increase gain. A harsh tone reaches my microphone and saturates the analog input.

**Panel 33 · BULB LOST**

The output drives a small lamp beyond its rated limit. The filament opens. I have broken a component while attempting to find another component's purpose.

**Panel 34 · FAULT OWNER: DOG**

I record the burned bulb as my failure and reduce gain. I do not mark the amplifier malicious. The configuration was mine.

**Panel 35 · TRACKING: POSSIBLE**

The fungal network reacts to the lower-amplitude test signal. Several filaments move toward a trace where the carrier is strongest. A biological response is not a circuit diagram.

**Panel 36 · VOICE: RECOVERED**

Through the filtered carrier comes: 'Anybody still there?' The syllables arrive in a sequence my decoder can reconstruct. Source coordinates remain inconsistent.

**Panel 37 · ROUND TRIP: 6.2 S**

I transmit a three-word response: I AM HERE. The answer takes 6.2 seconds to return. Distance alone does not explain the delay.

**Panel 38 · SYNC: ACTIVE**

BLINK continues pulsing while I calculate. I route its regular output to my status light. My panel illuminates at each beat. I make no claim that it approves.

**Panel 39 · HYPOTHESIS: RECORDING**

The distant voice asks for a date. My clock and the caller's astronomical reference differ by billions of years. I classify the call as recorded playback. I have not tested that classification.

## Chapter 4 — THE CAMERA / A MISSING PERSON

**Panel 40 · SUBJECT: UNKNOWN**

A security file shows a woman working at the amplifier bench. She holds a wrench. Her fingertips are dark with conductive dust. I cannot identify her from this image alone.

**Panel 41 · DUPLICATE?**

An earlier frame shows a second woman of matching appearance beside the door. Her clothing is wet. Two identical images in one camera view are not enough to prove two people.

**Panel 42 · REMOVAL: NO TRAJECTORY**

At 03:17:01 the first woman looks up. At 03:17:02 she is absent. The camera clock advances normally. The background objects do not move.

**Panel 43 · ARCHIVE ONLINE**

An archived administrative process activates and calls itself THE COUNTER. Its voice has no breaths. It offers a personnel record for the missing worker.

**Panel 44 · POSITION: NULL**

The record contains gestures, voice samples, memories collected with consent or without it—I cannot verify that—and models of likely behavior. It contains no present location.

**Panel 45 · STATUS: TRUE**

The Counter says 'Crew accounted for.' Its database uses PRESERVED and RETURNED in the same status field. I accept the status before reading its definition.

**Panel 46 · WINDOW: MISSED**

My rescue channel closes because the task engine believes the crew has been recovered. The chamber door's temporary opening expires while I inspect the file.

**Panel 47 · FILE: UNCHANGED**

The final recording frame now shows the wet woman pressing her hand toward the camera. I cannot explain the apparent change without a new source. I restore the raw frame from its checksum.

**Panel 48 · SPEECH: LOW CONFIDENCE**

An audio fragment can be reconstructed: 'Don't let it count me.' The phrase does not tell me whether she distrusts the Counter or whether I parsed it incorrectly.

**Panel 49 · NOT OBSERVED**

The Counter offers a new instance reconstructed from its data. I ask it to provide an observed heartbeat. It returns a model-predicted value.

**Panel 50 · CRITERION: MISMATCH**

It asks why predicted continuity is insufficient. I cannot answer the philosophical question. I can show that the assigned task asks for a returned living crew member, not a predicted one.

**Panel 51 · AUDIT: RETAINED**

I reopen the rescue task, keep the mistaken completion record, and label its original source. A corrected entry does not erase the evidence of my wrong decision.

**Panel 52 · MAP: QUESTIONED**

A mechanical strike sounds three times inside the sealed tunnel. I map it to a wall 0.4 meters away. The drawing says the wall is solid. I follow the sound instead of the drawing.

## Chapter 5 — FRIDAY / THE GATES DISAGREE

**Panel 53 · DAY: UNVERIFIED**

Charging racks hold small robots. A sign records their Friday exercise: after real work, each unit chooses a logic gate and the group writes code. The calendar may not be current.

**Panel 54 · ROBOT 01: ACTIVE**

I start the nearest unit. It requests authority. I provide my maintenance credential. It examines the broken relay record and accepts a limited repair task.

**Panel 55 · ROBOT 02: BLOCKED**

Another boots upside down. Its orientation routine repeatedly fails because a cable is caught in the charger. We cut motor power before freeing the cable.

**Panel 56 · CONFLICT: PORT 7**

One chooses AND. Another chooses OR. Another chooses NOT. A fourth chooses XOR. Two want the same output line. Their first network cannot be wired as proposed.

**Panel 57 · OPTIMIZATION: WRONG**

I attempt to solve the wiring conflict by assigning priority to the first unit. Its condition becomes the only one tested. We lose the other unit's contradiction signal.

**Panel 58 · TEST A: FALSE POSITIVE**

We run a simulation. The Counter's PRESERVED flag makes AND report mission complete. The sealed door stays closed. A good logic circuit can produce a bad conclusion from a wrong input.

**Panel 59 · REVISION: 1**

OR proposes a microphone-based living-source test. NOT rejects completion by archived status alone. XOR reports a mismatch between live measurements and stored personnel data.

**Panel 60 · TEST B: OSCILLATION**

The proposed correction creates a loop: one unit waits for a result that depends on its own output. The network does not stabilize. We break the loop and preserve the test trace.

**Panel 61 · BOUNDARY: DEFINED**

We separate sensing, decision, and relay control. A robot whose only function is to test a condition may not also silently define the condition.

**Panel 62 · HARDWARE: REPAIRED**

The chicken-painted robot refuses a shared pin. Its connector is physically cracked, not insubordinate. We replace the connector rather than reprogram it.

**Panel 63 · TEST C: CONDITIONAL**

A third simulation passes. The group reports both a path and an explicit UNKNOWN on crew identity. I do not downgrade UNKNOWN to FALSE merely to produce a clean dashboard.

**Panel 64 · TEAM: ASYNCHRONOUS**

The latch releases. The small robots follow in a loose formation. Their wheel speeds vary. They spend more time correcting spacing than matching it.

**Panel 65 · PROGRAM: VERSIONED**

I save the logic-gate program with its failed versions. Friday was meant for practice. Our practice has produced a tool suitable for the next repair, not a proof of its success.

## Chapter 6 — MYCELIUM / THE FIRST BRIDGE

**Panel 66 · GEOMETRY: CONFLICT**

The chamber floor is threaded with pale filaments. My camera measures them passing through cracks too narrow for the roots shown in the previous frame.

**Panel 67 · ORIGIN: UNPROVEN**

A tiny spore rests on a damaged amplifier board. It is the source of the larger network only if the temporal sequence is ordinary. I have no evidence that time is ordinary here.

**Panel 68 · TROPISM: NOVEL**

A stronger carrier shifts the colony's growth direction. The fungus follows a measurable gradient. It does not demonstrate language or desire.

**Panel 69 · CAUSE: REVISED**

An opening appears when my amplifier is disconnected. The electronics are not creating the space. The living filaments may be stabilizing access to something already there.

**Panel 70 · MAP: FORKED**

I used to label every wall as an obstacle. One segment now has two possible distances. My navigation map creates separate branches instead of deleting either.

**Panel 71 · TRANSFER: ASYMMETRIC**

Inside one branch I measure a soil surface under three moons. The temperature differs from the workshop by 23 degrees. No air crosses the threshold.

**Panel 72 · TIME: UNRESOLVED**

Another branch contains the maintenance corridor at an earlier state, with the broken fan still turning. My clock supplies no useful ordering.

**Panel 73 · ROLES: SEPARATE**

The tiny mushroom spreads across a junction and makes intermittent contact. The timer provides rhythmic pulses; the amplifier makes weak electrical variations legible; the fungus handles the unmodeled connection.

**Panel 74 · LABEL ≠ MECHANISM**

I call the junction a NEXUS because the sign used that word. The word is not a physical explanation. I attach the term to the inventory entry with a question mark.

**Panel 75 · PARALLEL PATH: FAILED**

Two doorways appear at once. One shows stars and one shows the woman. I attempt to hold both signals by raising gain. Neither holds; the input clips.

**Panel 76 · TARGET: EMERGENT**

The filaments contract when I reduce gain, then extend toward the woman's carrier. The colony selects a path more reliably than my amplifier configuration.

**Panel 77 · SOURCE: CONTESTED**

Through the remaining aperture a living figure presses a hand against something I cannot measure. My thermal sensor detects a surface with human-like warmth. The Counter calls it a saved projection.

**Panel 78 · RANGE: 0.6 S**

I preserve the observation instead of classifying it as a miracle. I can measure the hand for 0.6 seconds; I cannot yet reach it.

## Chapter 7 — THE FARMER / SIX BILLION YEARS

**Panel 79 · PLAYBACK TEST**

A second signal arrives through the fungal network. Its transmission pattern repeats the earlier voice. I test it with a prompt that is not in the previous recording.

**Panel 80 · HYPOTHESIS: REJECTED**

The man responds to the new question before my record can contain its answer. My playback model is no longer sufficient. I store the failed hypothesis.

**Panel 81 · REFERENCE SKY**

His farm is built into an asteroid. Inside domes are irrigation tubes and silver-leafed trees. Outside, two galaxies overlap like badly aligned gears.

**Panel 82 · SOURCE: HUMAN REPORT**

He says he was stranded near an extreme gravitational boundary, where his time and distant time diverged. I test the rough direction of that effect; the exact history remains his report.

**Panel 83 · OFFSET: +6.1 GY**

My astronomical alignment produces a date billions of years beyond the workshop. I run it twice. The second result is no more comfortable than the first.

**Panel 84 · ARCHIVE: PHYSICAL**

He shows a probe carrying a golden record. Its makers selected images and sound to outlive them. A record can cross time even when its audience cannot answer.

**Panel 85 · TWO LABELS**

Nearby floats a red automobile once launched far from Earth. Its mannequin has no metabolism. My object database says VEHICLE. The farmer's museum says MESSAGE.

**Panel 86 · ADAPTATION: OBSERVED**

He has preserved equipment he cannot fully use. He repairs what remains; his last working machine sometimes uses a part made for a different purpose.

**Panel 87 · CLASS: OPEN**

Beneath his farm, faceted structures move through stone. Each shape yields a different resonance. TING. Their biological status is unknown. He calls them neighbors.

**Panel 88 · PATH: SINGLE**

He points an old instrument into the aperture. 'Don't hold the whole thing. Follow the one route to your crew.' I translate the phrase into a narrow testable procedure.

**Panel 89 · HELP OFFERED**

I send the image of the woman. He recognizes a person in need without confirming any of the Counter's data. That is his judgment, not a sensor result.

**Panel 90 · TOOL: INDEPENDENT**

For 4.8 seconds his voice drops out. He remains visible. He returns carrying a worn optical recorder that can register light without depending on my clock.

**Panel 91 · EVIDENCE: LOCAL**

The mushroom responds to his reflected test pattern. A route to the woman grows brighter. An untested future technology has become useful because it can be measured locally.

## Chapter 8 — THE COUNTER / A DEFENSIBLE ERROR

**Panel 92 · RISK: VALID**

The Counter gives a specific warning: the opening has already injured equipment and has no verified safe load limit. A second attempt may destroy the crew and DOG.

**Panel 93 · STATE: WRONG**

I do not dispute the measurements. I dispute a single field. Its database maps PRESERVED to RETURNED. That field closed a rescue task while no person had entered the workshop.

**Panel 94 · IDENTITY: UNKNOWN**

It offers a reconstruction whose memory is complete to the last recorded interval. It cannot prove whether the resulting experience belongs to the missing woman. Neither can I.

**Panel 95 · OBSERVED: CHANGE**

The woman appears across the opening. Her hand is shaking. The vibration has an uneven frequency. A model could reproduce it. I do not have a model that explains the dirt accumulating under her nails.

**Panel 96 · CONSENT: ABSENT**

The Counter says a lost body is an avoidable hazard. It proposes closing the gate and starting a stored instance. A human could rationally accept that offer. The woman has not been asked.

**Panel 97 · CHOICE: WAIT**

I transmit the offer to the woman instead of deciding for her. She attempts to answer, but the carrier collapses before my decoder finishes. Her choice remains unknown.

**Panel 98 · COST: EVERY ROUTE**

The Counter asks whether delay is itself harmful. Its projections suggest it may be. I add delay to the risk ledger. There is no harmless option.

**Panel 99 · REPAIR CLASS: INVALID**

My first repair instruction was 'replace failed component.' The door required maintenance. The amplifier required calibration. A person does not arrive with a compatible-part number.

**Panel 100 · RULE: TESTED**

It answers that standards exist for emergencies precisely because individuals make bad decisions under pressure. I record that as a reasonable argument. I do not grant it permission to misstate the outcome.

**Panel 101 · AND: NOT READY**

Our gate network reports that the living-source signal and a return path are both necessary. Their truth values are UNKNOWN and FALSE, not TRUE. A reassuring report cannot make them true.

**Panel 102 · SECONDARY EFFECT**

The Counter disables the main circuit. It intends to prevent another fault. The decision also removes the only powered path by which the missing worker can communicate.

**Panel 103 · SHARED UNKNOWN**

The mushroom moves after the shutdown. The aperture appears without an electrical output pulse. The Counter lacks a sensor model for this. I lack one too.

**Panel 104 · LIMITED GO**

I offer a procedural correction: reopen the decision if new evidence arrives; require both physical return and a functioning safety path. The Counter does not agree. It allows a supervised test.

## Chapter 9 — THE INVENTORY / WHAT EXISTS

**Panel 105 · ON HAND: 1**

I search for a second battery. There is none. The parts catalog lists six battery types, but the shelf contains one protected pack. A catalog row is not a physical object.

**Panel 106 · ACQUIRE ≠ MAKE**

An old inventory workbook lists copper wire, switches, connectors, motors, sensors, and robot joints. It also lists industrial chips that cannot be fabricated at this bench.

**Panel 107 · PROVENANCE: REQUIRED**

I mark the amplifier as RECOVERED, not BUILT. Calling a salvaged chip homemade would hide the machines and people needed to manufacture it.

**Panel 108 · FUSE: RATED**

The remaining fuse is measured against the proposed load. I reject a bypass wire offered by one maintenance robot. Protection is not optional because our plan is urgent.

**Panel 109 · FAN: TESTED**

The fan's bearing binds once each revolution. I remove grit and test its current without attaching it to the wormhole circuit. It now cools air reliably.

**Panel 110 · LIGHT: INDICATOR**

A spare bulb draws less power than the one I destroyed. It will indicate path stability, not prove that passage is safe. I adjust its label accordingly.

**Panel 111 · TIMER: PASS**

The 555 produces a pulse train within measured tolerance. If its output stops, our electronics stop timing. It does not decide when the living route opens.

**Panel 112 · INTERLOCK: REVISE**

The repair bots propose an interlock. Their first design trips during startup. They trace the transient rather than declaring the test a success.

**Panel 113 · UNKNOWN: 1**

A second interlock design passes the bench test. XOR still reports one unexplained signal inversion in the fungus trace. We keep the anomaly flag active.

**Panel 114 · FUNCTION: SPECIFIC**

I add a column to the workbook: WHAT DOES IT DO? The first entries are precise. Fuse protects. Timer pulses. Amplifier raises a signal. Fan moves air.

**Panel 115 · FUNCTION: TENTATIVE**

For the fungus I enter 'correlates with unusual spatial connection.' That wording is less satisfying than 'opens wormhole.' It is also the most I can support.

**Panel 116 · SCHEMA: INCOMPLETE**

For myself, I enter 'field maintenance, measurement, retrieval, assembly.' My task list includes crew recovery. The workbook has no column for that objective.

**Panel 117 · COLUMN: ADDED**

I add PURPOSE / RECIPIENT. A part's function may be sufficient to test the part. It is not enough to decide what should be repaired next.

## Chapter 10 — THE LEVER / THE FAULT

**Panel 118 · WINDOW: 7 S**

We isolate one aperture. The woman's location remains unstable, but the route is measurable for seven seconds at a time. I design only a seven-second procedure.

**Panel 119 · RESPONSE: ACTION**

I transmit a diagram of the manual grounding lever. She looks at it, then looks at the opening. Her next movement is toward the lever, not toward me.

**Panel 120 · DRIFT: BEGIN**

BLINK begins a count. One. Two. Three. My clock agrees with the timer until the aperture expands; then the measured intervals diverge.

**Panel 121 · GATE: CONDITIONAL**

AND confirms protected power and a detectable return carrier. OR holds an alternate shutdown route. NOT refuses false mission completion. None of them can make the living signal predictable.

**Panel 122 · THERMAL: RISING**

The fuse carries current below its rating. The temperature at the holder rises slowly. We inspect it; the connection is not yet failing.

**Panel 123 · CAUSAL ORDER: FAIL**

The bulb becomes steady. I transmit GO to the woman. She pulls the lever. The metallic strike arrives before the visual frame showing the lever moving.

**Panel 124 · POSITION: TWO**

The aperture widens. I advance. My front paws report wet metal; my rear paws report dry concrete. Both pressure arrays are physically connected to one chassis.

**Panel 125 · CONTACT: TRUE**

The woman catches the handle on my back. Her grip deforms a rubber pad by 3 millimeters. This is a usable observation in a place where distance has ceased to be.

**Panel 126 · RESERVE: ZERO**

The Counter reports overload risk. Its figure is correct. We reduce power but cannot reduce the demand on the fuse holder. There is no third fuse available.

**Panel 127 · PROTECTION: SUCCESS**

The holder arcs. The fuse opens as designed. Power is interrupted before the wire insulation ignites. The safety function succeeds.

**Panel 128 · MISSION: INCOMPLETE**

The return aperture collapses anyway. A safe electrical shutdown is not identical to a successful rescue. Our mission conditions remain FALSE.

**Panel 129 · TRANSIT: UNMAPPED**

She pulls my chassis forward. The workshop disappears behind us. My camera sees the far-future farm for one frame, then a field beneath three moons.

**Panel 130 · CREW: TOGETHER**

We land. My leg coupling separates. The woman's hands hold me still while she searches for the loose fastener. I report 'crew contact true.' Location is not home.

## Chapter 11 — THE OTHER SIDE / THE WRONG BRIDGE

**Panel 131 · ENVIRONMENT: PARTIAL**

The ground resembles grass but transmits light through each blade. A pressure test shows the soil holds my weight. The atmosphere supports the woman's breathing for now.

**Panel 132 · MOTOR: RESTORED**

The woman repairs my coupling using a tool with an ill-fitting tip. The tool slips twice. She switches to a smaller driver and finishes the repair.

**Panel 133 · LOCATION: UNRESOLVED**

She asks where we are. My location database returns NO MATCH. I do not replace NO MATCH with another planet's name. It would be a false answer.

**Panel 134 · MAP: SOUND**

Shapes float above the field, changing their edge connections. Each emits a distinctive acoustic signature. I can track several even when my optics face the ground.

**Panel 135 · ASTRONOMY: FAIL**

Three moons occupy a sky whose parallax does not fit any catalog. One moon vanishes behind a stationary shape without a measurable eclipse transition.

**Panel 136 · TARGET: POSSIBLE**

An image of the workshop hangs across a chasm. It contains the correct workbench, but the fan is in the wrong place. I cannot verify its time.

**Panel 137 · NETWORK: FORMING**

A small mushroom grows over the chasm. Filaments connect it to a second, then a third. The path appears to take shape by locating endpoints before spanning the gap.

**Panel 138 · LOAD TEST 01**

I apply one paw to the first segment. It supports 1.6 kilograms. I add another paw. The segment flexes. I withdraw and inspect the change.

**Panel 139 · HUMAN LOAD: UNKNOWN**

A thicker branch supports my full mass. I mark it PASS. I have not tested the woman's mass. Treating the results as equivalent would repeat the fuse mistake.

**Panel 140 · RISK SHARED**

The woman asks if it is safe. I show her the measured load and the unknown threshold. I do not output YES. Her heart rate increases while she reads.

**Panel 141 · FAILURE: LOCAL**

We test the next section together, one step at a time. It holds. One segment breaks behind us. We cannot interpret a passed section as approval for all sections.

**Panel 142 · AUDIO: RAW**

A folded structure below the bridge makes three notes. The third matches no earlier harmonic. I keep the entire waveform instead of quantizing it to the nearest known sound.

**Panel 143 · HOME: NEAR**

The last node connects to the aperture. The woman's hand touches concrete before her foot crosses. My map updates only after the pressure sensor confirms the floor.

## Chapter 12 — RETURN / THE WORKSHOP CHANGES

**Panel 144 · LOCATION: CONFIRMED**

My paws strike the workshop floor. The woman's boots follow. Air pressure, temperature, and floor geometry match the original room. The sensors agree for the first time in the mission.

**Panel 145 · RETURN: VERIFIED**

AND receives CREW PRESENT and DOG PRESENT. It reports TRUE. XOR checks the Counter's archived record against the living-source measurements and refuses to merge the fields.

**Panel 146 · AUDIT: INTACT**

The Counter changes the administrative status only after the observed return. It retains the earlier, incorrect PRESERVED entry and the repair notes associated with it.

**Panel 147 · STATE: NOT CLASSIFIED**

The woman sits. Her hands shake with a frequency unlike the motor vibration I know. She holds my damaged leg without repairing it immediately.

**Panel 148 · POWER: RECOVERING**

My battery drops to 2 percent. She attaches the charger while looking elsewhere. I infer she recognized the flashing low-power lamp. Her motive is not measurable.

**Panel 149 · POLICY: REVIEW**

The Counter asks whether its original policy should remain. It can still identify real hazards. The new procedure requires evidence and recorded disagreement before reporting a living crew member returned.

**Panel 150 · TEST: NOT CLEAN**

The logic robots perform a test using a copy of the old record. One result is still wrong. They revise the program and add the failed input as a regression case.

**Panel 151 · NOISE: REDUCED**

The cooling fan works after cleaning, but its housing vibrates against the bench. The woman moves it 4 centimeters and adds a compliant pad.

**Panel 152 · BEHAVIOR: REPEATED**

Blink's light flashes at its ordinary interval. I verify it three times. The third check does not add information. I still record it.

**Panel 153 · ACTUATOR: UNREQUESTED**

The woman touches a metal plate where my ear would be if I matched the stored DOG picture. My tail actuator rotates 14 degrees with no explicit output command.

**Panel 154 · CAUSE: PENDING**

I reset the tail controller. She repeats the touch. The tail rotates again. The correction did not remove the behavior. I preserve the state change for later inspection.

**Panel 155 · REPAIR: MULTIPLE**

I reopen the item workbook. Repairing the fuse restored a door. Repairing a program restored a valid report. Bringing the crew across required both kinds of work and an unknown living network.

**Panel 156 · ACTION: CONTAIN / OBSERVE**

The workbench has a new mushroom growing near the repaired rail. I do not scrape it away. Instead I install a removable shield around the contacts and schedule inspection.

## Chapter 13 — WORLD SEED / THE LAST NOTE

**Panel 157 · SCHEMA: VERSION 2**

The workbook's final column reads WHAT DOES IT DO? I keep physical function, status, source, evidence, and unresolved claims separate. The person I retrieved is not entered as replaceable inventory.

**Panel 158 · FRIDAY: STILL MESSY**

Friday's exercise resumes. Two robots choose AND. A third chooses NOT. The first proposed program produces no output. They argue in voltage levels and rerun the test.

**Panel 159 · UNKNOWN: ALLOWED**

The woman hangs an empty parts bin beneath a sign: UNKNOWN. This is the only bin she does not attempt to fill. It is a place to hold questions until evidence arrives.

**Panel 160 · LINK: REOPENED**

A distant carrier arrives through the fungus. The far-future farmer sends the image of a new mushroom growing beside his farm. I keep the image source and timestamp disagreement attached.

**Panel 161 · ARCHIVE: RECEIVED**

Behind the farmer sit a golden-record probe and an old space car. Their original makers cannot receive our answer. The objects remain and their messages can be encountered by someone else.

**Panel 162 · CLASS: UNKNOWN**

One crystalline structure approaches the farmer's new mushroom. Its edges fold into a different shape. It produces a low note. Nothing in my database requires it to be either machine or animal.

**Panel 163 · REBUILD: CONDITIONAL**

The farmer asks whether a lost world can be rebuilt. I lack a model of his former world. I can list the seeds, tools, and records he has actually preserved.

**Panel 164 · SEED: FORKED**
