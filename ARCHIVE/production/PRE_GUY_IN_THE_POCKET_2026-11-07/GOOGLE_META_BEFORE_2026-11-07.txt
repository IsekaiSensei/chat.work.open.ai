### A. Design rule — gem is defined by the setting








- **Warm stones**: Garnet, Ruby, Citrine, Yellow Topaz, Yellow Sapphire, Padparadscha, Fire Opal, Carnelian — preferred warm frame language: yellow gold, rose gold, brass gold, rainbow titanium biased toward gold/rose/copper, bronze titanium.
- **Cool stones**: Amethyst, Aquamarine, Blue Topaz, Tanzanite, Sapphire, Turquoise, cool-cut Peridot — preferred cool frame language: white gold, platinum, silver, white titanium, blue/violet/teal anodized titanium, cobalt chrome, black rhodium.
- **Clear / neutral / high-brilliance stones**: Diamond, Clear Quartz, Moonstone, Pearl, White Opal — material setting defines mood: white metal = cool/icy modern; yellow gold = warm/vintage; rose gold = warm/romantic. Any can be used if the individual owner's reference requires it.
- **Construction lock**: clear crystal typically gets polished *white-metal external prongs/bezel/gallery* to preserve an icy optical look; put *yellow gold behind it as an internal reflector or skeleton* when warmth is required. **Gold inner skeleton + cool white exterior is a legitimate single look, not a continuity error.** Do not flatten them into one uniform yellow surface.
- **Setting geometry matters**: show ring/bezel/prongs, backing, halo, lattice, material transitions and crystal light return as distinct designed elements. A crystal floating in featureless yellow metal misses this canon.

### C. Preferred exotic metal/material library — use the full palette








- **Anodized titanium**: interference/oxide-layer appearance rather than paint/dye; multivoltage masked regions yield a rainbow/oil-slick finish. Owner's reference palette includes **~15 V straw gold; 25 V rose; 35 V deep blue; 55 V dark blue; 65 V purple; 75 V teal; 85 V gold-red; 100 V+ green**. These are *owner-specified visual labels*, not calibrated engineering guarantees. Prefer the pictured surface result.
- **Cobalt chrome**: bright, high-polish, slightly cool/bluish cast; cool backing and frame for blue crystal, aquamarine and blue topaz. High-luster modern finish.
- **Black zirconium**: opaque jet-black ceramic-like surface on heat-treated zirconium; useful as *cup/backing* that emphasizes a luminous or apparently floating crystal.
- **Niobium / tantalum**: deep blue/violet-colored-metal vocabulary with richer/saturated tonal options; use where shown/approved.
- **Damascus titanium / mokume-style patterned metal**: layered patterned surfaces; part of the owner's ancient-future masonry / modular-dragon material vocabulary. Preserve actual original patterns and fabrication geometry.
- **Traditional metals remain valid within the full palette**: polished yellow, white and rose gold; platinum; silver; brass/bronze; black rhodium; plus titanium, cobalt chrome, zirconium, niobium, tantalum. Mixed-metal bezels, under-galleries, hidden halos, internal skeletons and contrasting cups are intentional, not material mistakes.
- **Mixed-metal setting**: for example, deep-blue sapphire in *white-gold prongs* with a *blue-titanium under-bezel*; clear crystal supported by white-metal prongs but *warm gold reflecting from the interior*. This is a named preference, not permission to change an individual source's original design.
