# TO WORLD — Design Foundation: The Fairy Is the Cursor

**Book:** TO WORLD — Foundational Design Explanation  
**Version:** 1.0 (proposed opening; 2026-10-18)  
**Origin:** Ryan / Isekai Sensei, direct user explanation and correction, October 18, 2026  
**Status:** OWNER-DIRECTED DESIGN OPENING; staged for continuity QC and CO/XO review. Not a claim of shipped behavior or a merged canon release.  
**Core assertion:** **The Fairy is the cursor.**

## Read this first

The game begins at the interface between the human and the world.

The player's **Fairy is the cursor**—not a character who operates a separate cursor. The Fairy has a **hole that provides access to an infinite inventory**. Through the Fairy, the player learns a fundamental interaction sequence:

**CLICK → CONNECT → KEY → KEYSTROKE**

1. **CLICK:** point to and select something.
2. **CONNECT:** establish a relationship to something else.
3. **KEY:** associate an input with that relationship.
4. **KEYSTROKE:** perform that input and learn what it actually does.

**When the Fairy enters a world, the same Fairy becomes an in-world Fairy companion.** It is a transformation of role, not replacement by a second character.

The interface becomes a participant in the world it helps the player explore and shape. This is the opening explanation for the design: establish the relationship between **human → Fairy/cursor → connection → input → consequence → world**, then teach particular mechanics through that relationship.

## Why WASD belongs underneath this explanation

W, A, S and D are physical keys and a familiar movement arrangement, but they are not the first principle of this design. The first lesson is how a key acquires meaning through a connection, and what a keystroke does in a particular context. Default movement conventions and the existing DOG bindings can coexist with this larger interaction grammar.

## Reach across the world (design relationships, not implementation claims)

- **Player / input:** selection, connections, keyboard and controller bindings derive from the cursor interaction.
- **Characters / companions:** the in-world Fairy is the original cursor after world entry; her precise behavior and how the external pointer works afterward remain unspecified.
- **DOG and other roles:** interactions with machines, inspection, recovery and construction can be explained using the same interaction grammar. This does not replace or alter the already working DOG → FuseBox → Door loop.
- **Four domains / RPSV:** physical things and player verbs can be presented as things the cursor selects, connects and acts upon, without declaring their implementation complete.
- **Modular construction / World Seeder:** relations among modules, faces, edges, and world components could be operated through that same grammar; these extensions are conceptual until tested.
- **Wormholes / mycelium / fourth wall:** this premise gives the Fairy's interface-to-world transition a place alongside those previously described design ideas, without asserting a finished portal system.
- **The keyboard parable:** even a simple input can carry indirect consequences. This is a storytelling analogy, not a specified simulation rule.

## Important boundaries

- “Infinite inventory” is the user's description of what the Fairy's hole accesses. **Do not silently equate this with infinite free physical materials or production**; the detailed resource rules are unresolved.
- The exact entities connected, how binding works and what happens to cursor control after Fairy world entry are **OPEN QUESTIONS**.
- The Fairy is not inherently an external AI provider connection. Optional agent adapters and fairy appearances are treated separately in [Bring Your Own Agent — Fairy Identity and Art Direction](../FAIRY_BRING_YOUR_OWN_AGENT.md); basic play must not require a paid AI provider.
- This text explains design intent; it does not claim a functioning World Seeder, working new binding UI or revised Godot controls.
- Existing approved **Concept Page 001** remains Concept Page 001; its numbering, approved diagram and prominent first-page placement are not displaced by this explanatory introduction.
