## ONE ENVIRONMENT MASTER FOR FILM AND GAME
Build the forest as an actual reusable 3D level: terrain, paths, trees, landmarks and a stable coordinate system. The same environment supplies the game's navigable map and the film's rendered backgrounds.
Film views come from cameras and camera paths inside that level. Character travel is tied to the same routes and landmarks used for gameplay. A new camera angle does not generate a different forest.
Use layered foreground, middle-distance and background elements, parallax and foreground occlusion for the traditional cartoon effect the owner described. A background plate can pan while the character appears to walk; independent depth layers give the view more depth.
“PGN” in the dictation is implemented as a transparent PNG character asset. A walk cycle, pose sequence, separated articulated parts or rig supplies walking motion; one unanimated cutout translated across the screen supplies movement of the cutout, not automatically moving legs.
The character can be composited over a rendered background or placed as an animated sprite in the 3D scene. Match perspective, ground contact, scale and lighting to the master environment. Reuse the selected character asset to preserve its face, clothing and proportions.
The game uses actual level geometry, collision and navigation. Rendered plates are film outputs of that map. A generated forest image alone is a visual reference until a usable level has been constructed from it.
Keep original reference images unchanged; make derived transparency, pose, animation and compositing assets separately. This method retains the chosen visuals while allowing the same place to work on screen and in the game.
