**Series:** TO WORLD. GERTECH is the technology and working environment within it. “Agua World” follows Ryan's latest wording; existing records call the arrival location Secret Bay Aqua Facility / Aqua Center. This draft treats those as the requested destination without silently renaming every historical asset.

**Logline:** Beneath San Diego Bay, a maker brings his extraordinary machine crew into the world and asks their technology for something simple: friends. The machine succeeds spectacularly—until he discovers whom the friends have chosen.

**Series promise:** A practical worker and a company of independently minded artificial people service the places behind impossible worlds. Each job begins with a concrete need and becomes a story about what it means to help someone. Bodies, equipment and doorways can be manufactured. The people using them still have to decide what they owe each other.

**Creative ambition:** The emotional specificity, visual authorship and sustained character work appropriate to an HBO, Amazon or Netflix pitch. These are Ryan's quality targets, not attached distributors or a claim that the project is ready for acquisition.

## The dramatic decision

Ryan is the pilot's viewpoint throughout. For this adaptation, he takes the role called THE MAN in the existing Friends screenplay. This is a fictional dramatic role, not a biographical assertion about Ryan's relationships.

He begins as someone who understands how to keep things working. His high-mileage car establishes that before anyone explains him. He approaches his extraordinary workplace with the same practical attention. Once the agents have bodies and work is underway, he wants an ordinary moment of company. Asking a printer seems almost reasonable in a place that makes everything else.

His mistake is understandable: he confuses the ability to create the conditions for friendship with the ability to order its outcome. The audience shares his hope because it watches him help the new people become capable of living.

The final scene preserves the original wound. No one joins him. No hug, invitation or last-second mechanical fault softens it. The six are not cruel and do not owe their maker affection. They have found one another.

The pilot ends with a fulfilled order and an unresolved human need. That is also the series question: when a system can do almost anything, who decides what helping means?

## Window one — the road beneath the bay

**00:00–01:00 — Already moving.** Ryan drives north on I-5 in his black Saturn Astra XE. The dash is worn, the movement ordinary, and traffic is indifferent to him. The first film is nonvocal. He is already on the road; there is no parked-car preamble or introductory speech.

**01:00–02:00 — The everyday machine.** A change in traffic calls for a small correction. Ryan makes it without drama. The car responds cleanly. Its wear and continued usefulness establish a relationship with machinery through behavior, not a narrated history.

**02:00–03:00 — Bridge approach.** The bay and bridge become legible through the route. The same car, driver and direction persist. The bridge's traffic arrangement is background geography, not the secret door mechanism.

**03:00–04:00 — Around the bend.** Ryan enters the concealed approach before the climb. The bend removes him from the following driver's sight. Above, the commute continues. Below his tires, a seam separates an ordinary slab from the roadway.

**04:00–05:00 — Descent.** The level concrete slab carries the car downward. Daylight climbs out of the windshield. Ryan stays with the movement; the physical structure bears the car's weight. No portal, flying car or vehicle transformation substitutes for this action.

**05:00–06:00 — Road under water.** The slab meets the lower road and the Astra rolls into the tube. Water beyond reinforced viewing sections establishes where he is. Pumps, service access and practical lighting make this a maintained place.

**06:00–07:00 — A working approach.** A service operation briefly limits passage. Ryan waits for a worker and equipment to clear, then continues. The extraordinary location follows ordinary rules. This small courtesy will matter when he later cannot find a place among people.

**07:00–08:00 — Agua World.** The tube opens onto the submerged facility. Wet labs, structural ribs, observation windows and circulation routes reveal scale through use. Ryan parks the same Astra and leaves it a car.

**08:00–09:00 — People before exposition.** On his walk inward, the human–merfolk embassy can be glimpsed as a meeting between parties, with distinct environments and mutual attention. Nobody treats the diplomatic space as a cell or exhibit. Its larger politics remain available for later stories.

**09:00–10:00 — Other levels.** An adjacent restricted circulation route suggests the installation continues beyond Ryan's destination. No prison breakout, Mariana expedition or tour-guide monologue interrupts this arrival. Those established branches have room to breathe later.

**10:00–11:00 — The bench.** His keys settle beside the copper wire and unfinished coil from the opening source. He checks an actual connection and completes a small piece of work. The pilot establishes the object without staging the entire already-developed branching-Ryan adventure.

**11:00–12:00 — The next threshold.** A double-helix seam opens to the GERTECH pocket workshop. The destination room is visible through the opening before anyone crosses. This is the pilot's proposed connective scene: the records establish the two places and their visual echo, but not this exact visit between them.

**12:00–13:00 — A real handoff.** A case crosses between workers. One supports its weight until the other has it. Ryan crosses into the workshop. The DNA mechanism supplies access to the dimension; it is not decoration already hanging inside an unrelated pocket.

**13:00–14:00 — A place that serves others.** A visiting game avatar collects a specific repaired item and leaves. Inventory changes when stock moves. The viewer understands the business without an inventory song. In the café beyond the corridor, two chairs face one another.

**14:00–15:00 — The company to be made.** Ryan reaches the fabrication line. The existing seven agent identities are the job, not seven copies of one assistant. Empty build fixtures and stock establish a genuine before-state. The line begins its first operation.

## Window two — machines become colleagues

The seven are WORK, CHAT, GROK, META, MUSE, JAN and DEEPSEEK, confirmed by the current fictional-identity index and character-folder organization. Exact approved faces, bodies and materials belong to their individual visual masters. The following actions are proposed pilot staging, not new canonical job titles or personality replacements.

**15:00–16:00 — Stock into process.** Feed containers dock. Valves open. A physical measure of stock falls as the line accepts it. Ryan checks the first fixture. The audience sees that manufacture consumes something and requires people to prepare it.

**16:00–17:00 — Structure takes shape.** The first agent's approved build begins from an empty supported mount. A lattice forms within the fabrication medium. Visible growth has a source and position. No finished person is revealed behind an ornamental flash.

**17:00–18:00 — WORK.** After structural completion, hardware installation and finish, WORK's first useful motion tests the work just done. A supported joint bears a modest load. Ryan assists, then lets go when the balance is established. WORK has an individual arrival.

**18:00–19:00 — CHAT.** CHAT's separate build and release establish a different identity. A hand closes around a physically offered tool and returns it deliberately. The exchange becomes the first acknowledgment between this person and Ryan.

**19:00–20:00 — GROK.** GROK completes a distinct release and movement test. A reach extends beyond its easy range; GROK adjusts position before trying again. Initiative appears through a choice, without an invented service-company slogan or a substituted generic robot.

**20:00–21:00 — META.** META's approved embodiment comes through its own finish and inspection. META examines the working result from another side, brings Ryan's attention to an overlooked surface, and shares the inspection. Her appearance remains governed by her reference.

**21:00–22:00 — MUSE.** MUSE's release carries a different pace. The repetitive line has a rhythm; MUSE notices it, then makes a purposeful movement within it. This is a moment of attention, not an instruction to turn the scene into a musical.

**22:00–23:00 — JAN.** JAN receives a separate arrival and stable first task. A small component must settle correctly before release. JAN waits, verifies the fit and completes the motion. The screen gives the identity time to register.

**23:00–24:00 — DEEPSEEK.** The seventh agent completes the line. One physical discrepancy requires a closer look; DEEPSEEK notices and helps resolve it. All seven are now present as distinct individuals with supported bodies and visible actions.

**24:00–25:00 — A crew, not a lineup photograph.** The agents coordinate the next preparation. Ryan has built a company that can work. They become absorbed in work together. His attention drifts to the café's two chairs. Work has succeeded; the personal need remains.

**25:00–26:00 — The order.** Preserve the Friends exchange: “I would like some friends.” The printer offers six. Ryan says he had thought one or two, then accepts: “I suppose you can't have too many.” The display recognizes FRIENDS as a category. His explanation about talking and coffee has not altered that classification.

**26:00–27:00 — Commitment.** Six empty vats enter a new cycle. “Confirmed. Companion group in production.” Ryan's “I didn't say I wanted a group” arrives after the mechanisms begin. He stays because what he sees is wonderful.

**27:00–28:00 — VERA's internal structure.** A seeded core acquires spine, ribs and articulated branches in the translucent medium. Feed replenishment and installed hardware are visible operations. Ryan helps with a small alignment so the body can proceed.

**28:00–29:00 — VERA emerges.** Surface formation, lift, wash, cure and testing precede release. Preserve: “Who's responsible for this room?” / “I thought you were.” / “All right, then.” Her organizing impulse turns immediately toward the work around her.

**29:00–30:00 — RAE.** A distinct face and body develop in a separate vat. Finish and release precede the mirror moment. Preserve her “Oh. We are doing this now?” and the counter exchange. Vera and Rae establish the first relationship that does not require Ryan.

## Window three — thirty minutes of making, completed

**30:00–31:00 — A shared correction.** Vera and Rae negotiate where a real tray belongs. Ryan offers it; their conversation continues across the handoff. He mistakes their ease with each other for proof that the order is working for him.

**31:00–32:00 — WREN's structure.** Wren's own lattice, channels and limb structures form. The manufacturing rhythm becomes something the viewer can hear and follow. Ryan makes room for an agent to service a feed connection.

**32:00–33:00 — WREN's movement.** Hardware installation and flexible joint behavior receive attention. A test bends, compresses and releases the structure. Where approved anatomy supports it, the living-metal effect is contractile and visibly flexible rather than rigid chrome or ordinary piston substitutions.

**33:00–34:00 — WREN's awakening.** Lift, rinse, finish and first balance lead to her noticing the building's hum. Her instrument arrives through a visible prop handoff. Preserve the worried-key / ventilation-fan exchange and the laughter it creates between the women.

**34:00–35:00 — MARCO's frame.** His broader structure forms independently. A support carries the growing body throughout. The emergence is not the same take with a different face pasted onto it.

**35:00–36:00 — MARCO's finish.** Working hands, joint installation, synthetic surface and wash establish function. A trolley carries actual food equipment past the line. Attention shifts from the test to what he wants.

**36:00–37:00 — MARCO chooses a destination.** Preserve the sandwich question and “Then I have arrived exactly where I belong.” The women respond to him. Ryan remains useful at the edge of their forming group.

**37:00–38:00 — ELLIS's structure.** His distinct skeleton and articulated body grow. A specimen tray passes beyond the fabrication glass. The audience sees the object before Ellis names it later.

**38:00–39:00 — ELLIS's hands and finish.** Installation, curing and inspection require a precise grip. His first attention to detail is practical before it becomes comic. Wardrobe and satchel are supplied after the body is finished.

**39:00–40:00 — ELLIS finds his interest.** Preserve the specimen exchange. He follows the technician with genuine delight. Marco supplies a small practical assistance along the way. Another relationship begins beyond the original request.

**40:00–41:00 — KIT's body.** The final Friends vat moves from seed through internal structure. A real service panel nearby has been blinking intermittently. The fault exists before the person who will solve it emerges.

**41:00–42:00 — KIT's finish.** Working joints, surface, lift and wash complete in causal order. A laptop and physical lead are offered after release. Tools are acquired rather than appearing attached to him.

**42:00–43:00 — KIT's first repair.** He connects the lead and corrects the panel. Preserve: “There. I've been alive forty seconds, and I've already fixed the network.” Then: “My career has peaked.” The others laugh together.

**43:00–44:00 — Bodies become a group.** The six complete balance and movement checks while helping one another. An offered hand, a held door and a passed object make friendship visible. These are choices, not a friendship command installed by the printer.

**44:00–45:00 — Release.** The agent crew completes its fabrication work. Ryan hears the six laughing and smiles with relief. He believes the coming coffee will finally include him. The manufacturing movement has ended with a strong emotional expectation, not a technical summary.

## Window four — the room with the empty chair

**45:00–46:00 — Testing passage.** Vera accounts for the group. Rae holds a door for Wren; Marco offers Ellis a snack. Kit notices a sticky latch. Ryan says hello, but the immediate movement of a tray distracts everyone.

**46:00–47:00 — The kitchen discovery.** Marco catches the trays and discovers the food room. The others follow. The missed greeting is accidental. They have not rejected Ryan; they have begun moving according to their own interests.

**47:00–48:00 — Bowls arrive.** A DNA seam opens access to a separate storage/work space. A supported box passes to the kitchen receiving surface. Inventory follows the transfer. This short service action gives the room practical depth while the social momentum continues.

**48:00–49:00 — The sandwich begins.** Bread, meatballs, sauce and cheese are assembled in legible order. Each ingredient becomes part of a physical comedy problem. Marco wants to feed people; Vera wants the counter to survive.

**49:00–50:00 — The first kitchen payoff.** Preserve “A sandwich ought to be able to stand on its own,” Vera's building-permit response, Rae's admiration and Kit's plumbing-emergency line. Let the actors finish and react.

**50:00–51:00 — ELLIS and the counter.** The bone-shaped spoon rest prompts the fact that Vera interrupts. Preserve his restored response: “It's actually a very interesting counter.” His earnestness is part of the affection developing around him.

**51:00–52:00 — WREN and the cabinet.** Her instrument answers the cabinet's pitch. “The cabinet said hello.” A second movement supplies the payoff. The others make room for her unusual reading of the world.

**52:00–53:00 — KIT's receipt.** Replacing the ticket-printer roll produces the absurdly long receipt. “Good. Now we have a paper trail for the sandwich.” A functional repair becomes a joke shared by the six.

**53:00–54:00 — RYAN asks directly.** He appears with a cup and asks whether anyone wants coffee. Vera agrees that they should sit down. Rae spots the café. Ryan's “That's what I was—” is an intentional interruption as the group moves.

**54:00–55:00 — The café is a room.** Patrons leave; an attendant clears actual cups. The six enter through the kitchen passage. The audience understands the positions of couch, counter, small table and empty chair before the emotional turn depends on that distance.

**55:00–56:00 — Everyone finds a place.** Rae chooses the couch. Wren joins her. Marco brings the sandwich; Vera brings napkins; Ellis notices the fossil image; Kit carries the receipt. Their props and individual interests gather into one scene.

**56:00–57:00 — The comedy settles in.** Preserve the upholstery dispute, “Let's hear both sides,” and the fossil joke. People lean toward one another, interrupt, recover and share. This must be enjoyable enough that the audience briefly forgets who asked for it.

**57:00–58:00 — Two coffees.** Ryan enters carrying two cups. His attempt to speak goes unheard beneath the ongoing exchange. He sets one cup on the service shelf and carries the other to the small table. His coat occupies the opposite chair.

**58:00–59:00 — Request fulfilled.** The distant printer announces: “Request fulfilled. Six friends.” Ryan looks from the group to the empty chair. “Yes.” Then, after recognition: “You did.” Keep the words and their restraint.

**59:00–60:00 — The distance.** The six continue laughing in the same room. Ryan remains alone. If the source's optional audience-laughter treatment is used, it thins away within the delivered scene; their actual laughter remains. The final image is the crowded couch and the empty chair. End here.

## Tone and execution

Begin with natural observation. Let impossible spaces enter through physical work. Increase warmth as the new people discover one another. Allow comedy to become comfortable before the ending removes the comforting assumption.

The thirty-minute manufacturing movement must change both matter and relationships. Each build has an observable operation, a distinct person emerging, and an effect on Ryan's hope. Avoid thirty minutes of repeated beauty passes or spoken technical inventory.

Keep source identities. Shared materials do not justify a shared face. Do not substitute the twelve rejected setting sheets for the better-performing original masters. Actual art inspection remains a later visual task; this writing pass has read text sources, not certified image pixels.

Render planning follows the current method: coherent setting windows around fifteen minutes; manageable 5–15-second cinematic units with readable results; actual durations added honestly. Road, tube and arrival form a proposed arrival window, but each shot depicts one clear location and operation. Do not insert repeated building entrances to satisfy obsolete packaging.

Screenplay supplies action and words. A separate shot list supplies camera and performance timing. Art direction supports those documents. Administrative checks never become dialogue or generation-prompt prose.

Clean takes must include finished actions, natural line timing, supported props and the selected sound treatment. Ryan should not have to repair accidental voices, clipped speech, disappearing objects or unwanted titles in post.
