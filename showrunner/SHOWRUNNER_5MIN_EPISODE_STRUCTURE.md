# TO WORLD / GERTECH — DAILY FIVE-MINUTE TV FORMAT (NEW WRITING RULE)

**PRODUCTION LEVEL:** showrunner / story architecture. **This document is not a Meta render GO.** Owner-reported 40 usable videos stay completed and untouched; no assistant QC gate. This format applies to newly written material, never retroactively overwrites an accepted performance.

## The nested films — one story grammar at three sizes

- **1 second = microfilm:** one legible change: stimulus → human or machine decision → immediate consequence. Not a mandated one-second title card, frame effect, or separately generated video. Identify a decisive one-second event inside each ten-second take; optional standalone microfilm only when the owner selects one.
- **10 seconds = independent cinematic short:** a discernible situation, purposeful action, and outcome. It must be a true moving film, not a panned still; enough visual and musical space to breathe; no stitching/editing explanations or automatic last-frame inputs. **One owner-selected JPEG → one authored ten-second scene → Meta output → owner QC.**
- **5 minutes = one TV episode (30 × 10-second films = 300 seconds):** one complete dramatic argument with a musical through-line. The script gets its episode-level question, tension and resolution; the characters and world continue into tomorrow.
- **One daily episode:** three working batches of ten scene/JPEG pairs per episode, not a dump of 30 render requests at once. A day is the editorial/release target, not a claim that Meta will render on a schedule.

## College-essay outline mapped to TV acts (exactly 30 clips)

| Episode clip positions | Duration | Essay function | Television function | Contract |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 00–04 | 0:00–0:50 | Introduction: **five distinct thesis lines** | Teaser/Act I: five dramatic promises in song | Each line establishes one question, image, or stakes; not five variations on one theme. |
| 05–08 | 0:50–1:30 | Expand introduction **line 1** | Body A / first test | Four escalating actions, not four versions of the same repair. |
| 09–12 | 1:30–2:10 | Expand **line 2** | Body B / new discovery | A different discipline or perspective responds to line 2. |
| 13–16 | 2:10–2:50 | Expand **line 3** | Body C / complication | Demonstrable mechanical or dramatic resistance and meaningful choice. |
| 17–20 | 2:50–3:30 | Expand **line 4** | Body D / response | Consequence affects other characters or a larger environment. |
| 21–24 | 3:30–4:10 | Expand **line 5** | Body E / proof | One conclusive practical demonstration, not empty celebration. |
| 25–29 | 4:10–5:00 | Conclusion: **five echoes in transformed language** | Finale/tag: resolution + tomorrow's open door | Each of the five opening claims returns as a different, *earned* fact. No word-for-word restatement. |

This five-part body is an **original house structure** built from the owner's college-essay idea, not an industry-required 5-minute screenplay standard. TV formats vary: use established teleplay conventions for scene sluglines, character names, script action, act markers and the distinct shot list; do not claim 5 acts or 10-second takes are mandatory Hollywood rules.

## Standard film departments: never mix documents

1. **SERIES / EPISODE BEAT OUTLINE:** essay five-line thesis, escalating beats, payoff, episode question; no camera directions or render commands.
2. **SCRIPT — WORDS ONLY:** scene ID, speaking/singing character, exactly what is spoken or sung. No camera movements, editor's stitching notes, QC commentary, or inventory instructions. In a conventional teleplay, a separate action field may describe what is seen; for this Meta text-to-song handoff, physical and shot actions live in the next document, to honor the owner's writer/director division.
3. **SHOT LIST — CAMERA + ACTION ONLY:** time, exact JPEG ID, subject/action, framing and lens/movement, important one-second physical event, and audible *non-lyric* physical beat. No rewritten or repeated song text.
4. **BREAKDOWN / ONE-LINER / SCHEDULE:** cast, location, props, materials, practical tasks, source JPEG, production sequence.
5. **CALL SHEET:** only after the shot list and one-liner; belongs to the daily handoff.
6. **DAILIES / DELIVERY LEDGER:** render received, technical failure, source ID, user QC outcome **only as reported by the owner**. Do not auto-grade, hold, reject, replace, or rerender generated output.

## Continuous soundtrack, not a repetitive inventory song

- Compose one **complete 300-second sung performance** (or a properly authorized existing master) across the entire episode. The 30 ten-second excerpts are scene *sections*, not 30 restarted songs. Existing approved singers and exact lyrics remain unchanged. The score can evolve by section through register, rhythm, instrumentation, harmony and pauses; no musical reset at clip boundaries.
- Write **five introduction lines first**, one for each claim. Then develop each line through the corresponding four-scene body paragraph. Finish with five transformed echoes. Lyrics need internal tension, surprise, humor and emotion, not repeated gear names or repeated declarations that the work continues.
- Let objects and machines supply sound; do not make the dialogue describe an object already obvious on screen. Spoken noun should be in hand or present in the shot where practical (owner's noun-in-hand rule).
- Do not substitute a new tune or lyrics for an accepted existing performance. A new verse/song is a **new draft for a future episode** only.

## Repetition rule — writer-facing lexical ledger (never a render gate)

- Track only **actually sung/spoken words**, in time order, not camera descriptions, HUD text, captions, document headers, or song credit metadata.
- **Do not use “I’m building,” “I'm building,” “we're building,” or generic variants as filler.** Avoid *build / built / building* and *lattice* in new sung dialogue unless an owner-selected tangible item is **being held/shown in a genuinely NEW revision** and at least **50 other spoken words** separate it from the prior same spoken root. This is the owner's threshold, not a reason to censor old accepted performances.
- Extend a **50-other-spoken-word watch** to other conspicuous words and repeated phrases (e.g. crystal, gold, frame, system, power, scan, again, connected, a little part), unless intentional refrain is specifically approved by the owner. Track lemma/stem where appropriate. Never relabel random synonyms as fresh story progress: compare the *physical dramatic action* as well.
- Flag repeated 3–6-word strings, repeated end-rhyme clichés, recycled “something fails → I adjust → green/blue stable” endings, and word-inventory chanting. Rewrite **future drafts only**, maintaining sense, meter, speaker identity, syllable load and physical noun-in-hand.
- If a requested exception is warranted, document: **spoken word, previously used in which clip, at least 50 intervening new spoken words, item in hand/shot, new revision demonstrated**. Do not invent a fabricated prop or revision to justify repetition.
- Writer scans are text-edit assistance, **not production permission to withhold video**. Successful Meta output always goes to Ryan for his own QC.

## Production image / numbering / ownership

- New window counts start at 000. The owner reports about 40 usable renders in this window; preserve them. **If those occupy 000–039**, 040–069 and 070–099 supply two 5-minute episodes. This conditional numbering does not assert a verified Meta receipt or reopen any completed shot.
- Each ten-second shot uses **its exact selected source JPEG**. The locked gold-geodesic crystal workshop and real face/visor masters inform visual direction, but are not assistant preflight pass/fail gates. Old MP4 end-frames cannot replace selected JPEGs by default. Do not generate replacement source images merely because a production sheet was shown.
- **One hundred shots do not automatically equal 100 minutes.** They total 16 minutes 40 seconds; three complete five-minute episodes plus ten remaining ten-second films. Daily broadcast cadence is an editorial goal and requires the owner's actual render/assembly/publish decisions.
