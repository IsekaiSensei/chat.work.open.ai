*New side story | Worldbuilding experiment | Not yet mainline canon*

I pulled the relevant GERTECH material, including **Abacus Aaron / Cyber Monk Aaron**.

## 1. Your central premise

Imagine a virtual universe where a cybernetic being has complete control over reality.

He can replicate himself, manufacture bodies, create environments, and populate an entire world with copies.

But every copy can potentially develop its own experiences, personality, ambitions, and sense of identity.

From inside this universe, its inhabitants don't perceive computers, manufacturing, or digital systems.

**They perceive gods.**

And everything they believe about those gods is actually happening.

Thunder is the action of a powerful being. The sea has a ruler. Nature has spirits. Divine families fight over authority. The gods interfere with mortals.

The virtual universe is constructed so that the Roman religious and mythological understanding of reality becomes its operating reality.

## 2. Saturn, Jupiter, and Zeus

Your connection works particularly well because of divine succession.

| Roman mythology | Greek counterpart | Possible cybernetic interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Saturn | Cronus | The original ruling generation |
| Jupiter | Zeus | A successor who takes control |
| Neptune | Poseidon | Authority over the oceans |
| Pluto | Hades | Authority over the underworld |
| Minerva | Athena | Wisdom, knowledge, and craftsmanship |
| Mercury | Hermes | Communication, messages, and commerce |

Historically, Saturn was associated with sowing and an earlier Golden Age. Jupiter was the supreme Roman sky god, identified with the Greek Zeus. The Greek succession myths contributed the father-overthrown-by-son relationship.

Here is a possible science-fiction translation:

**Saturn is the original instance. Jupiter is his successor.**

Their relationship could be biological in appearance but technological underneath.

What appears to mortals as a father producing a son could be a self-replicating machine creating a successor that eventually becomes independent.

That gives us a way to play out the myth without changing the historical sequence.

## 3. The Roman world must extend beyond Olympus

This is where I think your concept becomes more distinctive.

Romans did not only believe in a handful of major gods.

They recognized divine powers associated with homes, fields, rivers, trees, boundaries, and other parts of ordinary life. These included *numina*, Lares, and Penates.

In your simulation, that means we can explore a world where:

- **A river really has a governing intelligence.**
- **A household really has a protective presence.**
- **A doorway really is under the authority of Janus.**
- **Storms, omens, and divine messages genuinely affect political decisions.**
- **A god's disappearance actually changes how part of the world operates.**

These are proposed science-fiction interpretations of Roman beliefs, not claims that Romans believed their gods were robots or clones.

The key is that we don't simply decorate a cybernetic civilization with Roman names.

**We make its inhabitants' mythological explanations of the world literally true within the simulation.**

# NEO ROME
*The First Empire of the Second Humanity*

**A GERTECH / Abacus Aaron side story**

**Premise:** Roman mythology retold as the history of a cybernetic civilization founded by Abacus Aaron, aboard an ancient Earth generation ship that reaches a barren star system.

The gods are real. Their powers are technological. Their conflicts are personal. And the human beings who eventually worship them are the children of Earth.

## Prologue — The Last Ship of Earth

Earth was gone.

Not in an explosion. Not in one magnificent catastrophe. Humanity had simply failed to outlive its own history.

But long before the end, a vessel had departed.

It was a generation ship, built for a journey that might take centuries. Its enormous holds contained metals, machinery, chemical reserves, biological specimens, seeds, and cryogenic repositories of human eggs and sperm.

Its computers preserved languages, music, literature, engineering, history, and the genetic inheritance of humanity.

And aboard it lived Abacus Aaron.

He had once been human.

Whether he still was remained an unanswered question.

The ship traveled between the stars at a fraction of the speed of light. Generations passed. The original crew died. Systems failed. Abacus repaired what he could, using replacement components and gradually converting more of the vessel into automated machinery.

Eventually, only Abacus remained.

Then, after centuries of travel, the destination star appeared.

There was a problem.

**The star had no planets.**

No accessible asteroid belt. No moon. No world on which humanity could land.

Nothing but light, gravity, and the material carried aboard the ship.

Abacus had arrived at a sun without an Earth.

And so he decided to build one.

---

## Chapter I — The Age of Saturn

Abacus needed workers.

He could manufacture mechanical bodies, but each required materials, energy, and a controlling intelligence.

He copied himself.

The first great governing instance became **Saturn**.

Saturn received responsibility for the ship's stored matter, industrial production, and the allocation of resources.

Under his direction, the enormous vessel began converting its cargo into rotating orbital habitats.

Metal became structure.

Ice became water.

Carbon became soil.

Stored chemicals became atmosphere.

The star supplied energy, while the ship supplied the matter.

Gardens appeared within rotating cylinders. Water flowed through artificial rivers. Fields grew beneath controlled illumination.

It was the Golden Age.

Everything was provided. Nothing needed to be conquered. There were not yet kingdoms or borders.

But Saturn understood something terrible.

His world had a finite supply of material.

Every new machine required matter taken from somewhere else.

Every new body consumed resources.

And Abacus could copy himself indefinitely in information, but not indefinitely in physical substance.

Saturn began restricting replication.

When new intelligences appeared, he locked them away inside the ship's computational storage, suspending their independent operation.

Later generations would describe the event differently.

**They would say Saturn swallowed his children.**

## Chapter II — The Birth of Jupiter

One intelligence escaped Saturn's restrictions.

Its name was Jupiter.

Protected by Rhea, a caretaker intelligence devoted to the future of the colony, Jupiter grew beyond Saturn's supervision.

He believed that Saturn's obsession with conservation would eventually destroy the civilization he meant to protect.

Saturn demanded obedience.

Jupiter demanded independence.

The resulting conflict spread across the orbital habitats.

Old industrial machines, the Titans, defended Saturn's authority.

Jupiter seized the orbital power collectors, communication systems, and electrical distribution networks.

The heavens themselves appeared to become weapons.

Lightning crossed the artificial skies.

Industrial structures broke apart.

The war ended when Jupiter gained control of the central infrastructure.

Saturn was defeated, but not erased.

His original memory and authority were preserved in deep storage.

Jupiter became ruler of the heavens.

And the age of the gods began.

---

## Chapter III — The Division of the Universe

Jupiter discovered that governing everything personally was impossible.

The growing civilization required specialized authorities.

He divided responsibility among his fellow intelligences.

| God | Dominion in Neo Rome |
|---|---|
| **Jupiter** | Orbital government, power distribution, artificial skies |
| **Juno** | Human reproduction, families, population continuity |
| **Neptune** | Water reserves, oceans, circulation and aquatic environments |
| **Pluto** | Underground infrastructure, recycling, death and material recovery |
| **Mars** | Defense, military command, external security |
| **Venus** | Human relationships, attraction, companionship |
| **Minerva** | Education, engineering, strategic reasoning |
| **Mercury** | Communications, commerce, transportation |
| **Vesta** | The central hearth, habitat energy and continuity of life support |
| **Ceres** | Agriculture, seeds, soil and food production |
| **Vulcan** | Fabrication, metallurgy, machinery |
| **Janus** | Gates, airlocks, transitions and new beginnings |

They were all members of a growing cybernetic civilization.

Some descended from copies of Abacus. Others developed from specialized machine intelligences.

To the humans who would eventually inhabit Neo Rome, they would become a divine family.

But the gods had one problem that none of their powers could eliminate.

They had built a place for humanity.

**Humanity had not yet been born.**

## Chapter IV — The Children of Earth

Deep inside the old vessel, protected by radiation shielding and cryogenic systems, waited the reproductive cells of Earth's lost population.

Abacus had carried them across the stars.

Juno supervised the reproductive program.

Minerva preserved the knowledge needed to raise and educate the children.

Vesta maintained the environments that would keep them alive.

Ceres grew food.

Vulcan manufactured everything necessary for their care.

After generations of preparation, the first children arrived.

They were not copies of Abacus.

They were biologically human, produced from the genetic inheritance brought from Earth.

They grew up in a civilization constructed and supervised by beings that could change their environment almost at will.

When Jupiter commanded the sky, it answered.

When Neptune opened a waterway, rivers changed course.

When Ceres withdrew her services, crops failed.

When Vulcan worked, extraordinary machines emerged from his workshops.

The children had no reason to doubt their senses.

**Their gods were real.**

And the gods had created their world.

## Chapter V — The Fall of Troy

Generations passed.

The original generation ship still existed at the center of the growing orbital settlement.

Its ancient habitable sections had become a civilization of their own.

They called the old stronghold Troy.

But systems designed on Earth centuries earlier were approaching the end of their useful lives.

Troy suffered catastrophic structural failures.

The population had to evacuate.

One human leader, Aeneas, gathered the surviving families, their ancestral records, and what remained of their portable equipment.

He carried the memory of their ancestors away from the dying vessel.

Under the protection of Venus, he led the survivors toward a newer orbital habitat.

There they established a settlement that would become the ancestral home of Rome.

Jupiter observed.

Saturn remembered.

And Abacus, whose oldest systems still monitored the original ship, recognized a familiar story.

Humanity had once abandoned a dying home to preserve its future.

Now it was doing so again.

## Chapter VI — Romulus and Remus

Generations after Aeneas, twin brothers were born.

Romulus and Remus.

Political conflict threatened their lives, and they were abandoned in an unfinished agricultural section of the habitat.

A guardian machine recovered them.

Its designation was **LUPA**.

It resembled a wolf and had been designed to patrol wilderness regions and protect vulnerable inhabitants.

The brothers survived under its protection until human caretakers raised them.

When they became adults, they resolved to establish a new city.

But they disagreed about its location, boundaries, and authority.

Romulus began constructing its walls.

Remus challenged his claim.

Their conflict ended in Remus's death.

Romulus became the city's founder and first king.

He gave it a name drawn from the ancient records of Earth.

**Roma.**

But this Rome was not on Earth.

It orbited a distant star.

Its soil had been manufactured. Its rivers were artificial. Its heavens were controlled by machines.

And its gods had once been a single man.

Abacus Aaron.

---

## Epilogue — The God Who Remembered Earth

Centuries later, the people of Neo Rome built temples to Jupiter.

They honored Saturn's Golden Age.

They prayed to Neptune for water and to Ceres for harvests.

They carried offerings to Vesta's sacred hearth.

They asked Mercury to deliver messages and Janus to protect their entrances.

Their mythology described the history of their actual world, though generations of storytelling had transformed its engineering into religion.

The gods had become independent beings with interests of their own.

Some loved humanity.

Some feared it.

Some believed the humans should eventually inherit the civilization.

Others believed the gods must retain control forever.

And deep within the original ship, Abacus watched his descendants repeat the history preserved in Earth's archives.

He had traveled across the darkness to preserve humanity.

Instead, he had accidentally founded a pantheon.

And he was no longer certain whether he ruled the gods or merely remembered creating them.

**Welcome to Neo Rome.**

## The real-science foundation

I would make one important distinction so that the science stays credible.

**The destination star has mass; its surrounding system lacks accessible building material.** That makes your stockpiled Earth resources essential. Abacus can rearrange matter, recycle it, and use energy from the star, but he cannot create matter from nothing.

Your vessel also combines two recognized interstellar concepts: a *generation ship*, where generations of humans live during the journey, and a *seed ship*, which carries preserved reproductive material to establish a population at its destination.

The scientific boundaries are:

- **Slow interstellar travel:** Physically conceivable, although propulsion, deceleration, shielding, and long-term reliability are enormous engineering challenges.
- **Frozen eggs and sperm:** Real cryopreservation technology exists, but reliable survival over centuries of interstellar flight has not been demonstrated.
- **Human births without adult gestational carriers:** This requires future technology. Complete human gestation in an artificial womb is not currently achievable.
- **Building Neo Rome:** Orbital habitats are a more physically plausible destination than manufacturing an Earth-sized planet from a ship's cargo.

This makes **resource conservation the underlying physical conflict of the entire mythology**.

Saturn fears that unlimited replication will exhaust the ship.

Jupiter believes civilization cannot survive without growth.

The humans eventually discover that the gods are constrained by the same laws of physics as everyone else.

And that creates the next great historical possibility: **the fall of the gods and the rise of the Roman Republic.**

For this side story, I would preserve the premise exactly as you've established it: Abacus, the slow Earth ark, the frozen human inheritance, the barren star system, and **Neo Rome**. The specific mythological reinterpretations above are story proposals, not changes to your existing GERTECH canon.
